Frequently Asked Questions

Some common questions we are often asked

Everything you need to know about caring for your plants, botanical adaptations, and shipping policies.

Roots serve multiple essential functions for plants. They anchor the plant in the soil, absorb water and minerals, and also store reserve food materials to ensure strong, resilient growth during dry spells.

Plants exhibit various adaptations, such as altering their leaf shapes, developing extensive root systems, or having waxy coatings, to survive in different environments, coping with factors like temperature, water availability, and soil type.

C3 plants (like wheat and rice) and C4 plants (like corn and sugarcane) differ in their carbon fixation pathways. C3 plants use the Calvin cycle exclusively, while C4 plants use an additional step before the Calvin cycle which makes them much more efficient in high temperatures.

Plants communicate through chemical signals and symbiotic interactions with other organisms. They release compounds into the air or underground fungal mycelium networks, warning neighbors against pests and regulating root spacing.

Common plant diseases include powdery mildew, root rot, and various leaf blights. Prevention involves maintaining proper plant hygiene, ensuring good air circulation, watering the roots rather than foliage, and using natural neem oil treatments.

Pollinators, such as bees, butterflies, and hummingbirds, aid in the transfer of pollen between plants, facilitating cross-pollination. This process is essential for the production of healthy fruits, flowers, and seeds across ecosystems.
